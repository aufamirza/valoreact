Why Establish an Aim Baseline?

Consistent FPS performance begins with knowing your baseline. Random practice without benchmark metrics makes progress hard to verify. Our Basic Training runs three core drills with standardized parameters so you can measure raw mechanical skill, identify weak points, and track real progress over time.

Key Benefits & Design Philosophy

Zero Barrier to Entry

Jump directly into the arena from any modern browser. No account registration, client downloads, or subscriptions required.

3 Core FPS Disciplines

Evaluates micro-flicking (Precision), fast target acquisition (Double Shot), and continuous smoothness (Target Tracking).

Standardized Conditions

Consistent target sizes, spawn speeds, and 60-second round durations guarantee your results are comparable session after session.

Universal Game Transfer

Mechanics translate directly to Valorant, Counter-Strike 2, Apex Legends, Overwatch 2, and any competitive shooter.

Guidelines for Accurate Benchmarking

Match Your In-Game Sensitivity

Configure your browser aim trainer to use the exact same eDPI and resolution ratio as your main competitive FPS title.

Maintain Neutral Posture

Keep your forearm resting naturally on the mousepad, sit upright, and ensure your wrist has unimpeded range of motion.

Prioritize Accuracy Over Wild Flicks

Aim for high precision first; speed naturally follows clean mechanics. Overflicking only introduces inconsistent muscle memory.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to sign in or pay to use Basic Training?

No! Basic Training is completely free and requires no sign-up. You can jump in and train right in your browser. If you choose to log in later, your future training sessions will automatically sync to your cloud history and leaderboard.

How often should I do the Basic Training routine?

We recommend doing this baseline routine once at the beginning of each training day or 3-4 times per week as an objective benchmark test.

What difficulty should I pick?

Medium is the calibrated standard matching typical competitive FPS hitboxes. If you are new to aim trainers, start on Easy; experienced players can test on Hard.

Does this help in Valorant and CS2?

Yes. Valorant and CS2 heavily rely on micro-adjustments for headshots, angle clearing flick speed, and tracking enemies who counter-strafe. These 3 drills isolate those exact motor skills.
